Background Grammar  
The English Language Curriculum adopts a contextual and continuous approach to grammar learning that 
previously learnt structures are reused in the context of new themes. A revision programme, implemented 
at the beginning of each academic year, is designed to reuse all grammatical knowledge from earlier years 
by recalling and consolidating them in context. In addition to this, it provides opportunities for students 
to encounter and reuse earlier content, supporting the acquisition of new target structures. Rather than 
reteaching these prior elements explicitly, they are embedded naturally within the context of some theme, 
functioning as supportive features alongside new grammatical targets as context and meaning makers. New 
or previously learned, grammar is not taught through isolated or direct instruction, instead, students are 
encouraged to understand and internalise grammar through meaningful use and contextual exposure by 
exploring and discovering their functions, meaning, and use.
Integration of Idiomatic Everyday Social Language Expressions
The aim of including “idiomatic social language expressions” used in real-life situations in the English Language 
Curriculum is to build a connection, appropriate to the students’ age, between the language learned in the 
classroom and the language used in real life. The goal of using everyday idiomatic social language expressions 
in every theme and year of the English Language Curriculum is to add “currency (up-to-dateness), relevance, 
fluency, authenticity (realness), and meaningfulness” to the language. In this way, it is intended to develop 
students’ ability to use the language in different social contexts, to understand and learn about different 
socio-cultural uses of language, to communicate with speakers of the language through these different 
socio-cultural uses, to develop respect for diverse socio-cultural uses, to activate emotional and social 
intelligence during language use, and to use the foreign language in its natural form. The aim is for students 
to enrich, internalise, and use the foreign language they are learning more naturally.
Differentiated Learning 
Differentiated Learning is one of fundamental components of the English Language Curriculum of the 
Century of Türkiye Education Model. In the English Language Curriculum, differentiation activities which are 
held as expanding and supporting activities have been designed by taking the main activities in “learning-
teaching experiences” section as central. They are designed by considering the diverse needs of learners 
to provide a fair and equal learning opportunities to all learners in harmony with their pace and capacity. 
The design and modification of differentiated activities has been organised in terms of their content and 
process. Expectations for the end products have been varied since learners’ performance could be different. 
The environment of learning has also been considered to provide a physically and socially convenient and 
comfortable milieu for learners. Activities have been carefully graded to achieve the provision of effective 
and appropriate techniques for a variety of learners with diverse needs.
Integration of Technology into the English Language Curriculum   
Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge (TPACK) is known as a framework to integrate technology 
into educational programmes in an effective way for teachers to be aware of three major knowledge types: 
technological, pedagogical, and content knowledge. A significant part of the technological stance of the 
English Language Curriculum has been designed around the three pillars of this framework: helping teachers 
recognise what they already know about the language (grammar, vocabulary, phonological aspects, etc.); 
recognising how much they know about how to facilitate learners’ learning (lesson planning, techniques, 
etc.) and how they can use educational technologies and tools (AI based tools, Internet platforms, etc.); to 
expand learners’ engagement, and deepen their understanding. The conscious use of technology to improve 
learning-teaching experiences and practices are supported in this way through several apps for designing 
materials such as digital stories, summaries, picture strip stories, documentaries, songs, poems and so on
