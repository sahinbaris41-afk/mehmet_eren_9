Generalisations/Key Concepts  
Generalisations
The target concept of this theme ''family traditions, values, and customs (watching films together, 
preferences for films, music, and sports); family problems related to sharing a house and solutions'' is 
present in almost all languages. Students who start to learn a new language inevitably bring with them their 
pre-existing knowledge and transfer it to their new language learning context. For example, a student in an 
upper secondary school may know what ''family traditions, values, and customs (watching films together, 
preferences for films, music, and sports); family problems related to sharing a house and solutions'' means 
but may not know how to say these things in another language if this has not yet been covered in their existing 
curriculum. However, this existing knowledge in their own language will help them recognise, understand, 
and learn the new information more easily in a foreign language, in this case English.
Key Concepts
Family traditions, values, customs, habits (watching films together, preferences for films, music, and sports); 
family problems, family problems related to sharing a house, solutions to family problems. 
INDICATORS FOR LEARNING (Assessment and Evaluation) 
PERFORMANCE ASSIGNMENT: Students will create a project titled ''Family Dynamics and Shared Living 
Spaces'' exploring the progress of family life and its challenges across time and cultures. They will begin by 
describing the daily routines of their family members, comparing these to those of previous generations to 
show how roles and responsibilities have changed, using sentences like: ''Parents had taken full responsibility 
for chores before children started helping''.  Next, they will focus on family traditions, hobbies, and interests, 
such as favourite sports or films, explaining how these activities strengthen relationships and allow for 
personal expression. Finally, students will research different types of families around the world, comparing 
them to their own experiences and reflecting on how cultural differences shape family life and shared living 
spaces. The project should include creative elements, such as interviews, photos, drawings, or videos, and 
will be presented in class. The assignment will be evaluated by using a rubric scheme, a rating scale, and/or 
a marking scheme.
