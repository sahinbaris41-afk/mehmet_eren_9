Key Concepts
School life, education, different types of schools in the world; regular and extracurricular activities at different 
types of schools; school anxiety and stress; national days and celebrations.
INDICATORS FOR LEARNING (Assessment and Evaluation) 
PERFORMANCE ASSIGNMENT: Students create a comprehensive project titled ''School Life and Education'' 
that focuses on the themes of different types of schools in the world, regular and extracurricular activities, 
and school anxiety and stress. Students begin by researching and describing various types of schools 
around the world, such as public schools, private schools, boarding schools, and alternative schools, 
highlighting their unique characteristics. Next, they explore both regular activities, like lessons and exams, 
and extracurricular activities, such as sports, arts, and clubs, emphasising how these contribute to student 
development. Additionally, they address the challenges students face, including school-related anxiety and 
stress, and propose strategies or activities that help overcome these issues. The project should include 
creative elements, such as charts, or videos, and students will present their findings in class. The assignment 
will be evaluated by using a rubric scheme, a rating scale, and/or a marking scheme.
LEARNING-TEACHING EXPERIENCES
Basic Acceptations: Preparedness/Readiness 
This theme includes the concept of ''school life and education with its components such as different types 
of schools in the world; regular and extracurricular activities at different types of schools; school anxiety 
and stress; national days and celebrations'' and it is assumed that it could be present in almost all languages, 
with some exceptions. It is assumed that a student in an upper secondary school may know what school life 
and education, school types, activities and events at school mean in their own language as it is known that 
almost all people who start to learn a new language bring their pre-existing knowledge (in their own language) 
with them and transfer it to their new language learning context. However, they may not know how to say 
these things in another language if this has not yet been covered in their existing curriculum. Fortunately, this 
existing knowledge in their own language can help them recognise, understand, and learn them more easily 
in a foreign language, in this case English. 
As they are students of an upper-secondary school in year 10, they are expected to know some basic 
vocabulary about ''school life and education'' in English from previous themes or years such as ''a teacher, a 
student, a headmaster, a class, a classroom, to study, to go to school etc.'' and also some structures such 
as ''What’s/is it/this? It’s/is a classroom. What do students do at school? They study and they learn? What 
are they learning now? They are learning about the people in the school now.'' in English from the previous 
themes and/or years. These grammar items and vocabulary are revised during the ''Revision Programme'' at 
the beginning of the year and they can be used as a basis for learning the new vocabulary and grammatical 
items in the current theme, so that the new grammatical elements in the current theme can be learnt easily.
