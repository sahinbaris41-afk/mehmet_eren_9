Generalisations/Key Concepts   
Generalisations
The target concept of this theme ''life in the neighbourhood, city & social life with its components such 
as activities to do in attractions and recreational places (cafes, cinemas; gyms, sports clubs); getting 
help from services (pharmacies, hospitals, etc.); pros and cons of different types of transportation in the 
neighbourhood and city'' is present in almost all languages. Students who start to learn a new language 
inevitably bring with them their pre-existing knowledge and transfer it to their new language learning context. 
For example, a student in an upper secondary school may know what ''life in the neighbourhood, city & social 
life with its components such as activities to do in attractions and recreational places (cafes, cinemas; gyms, 
sports clubs); getting help from  services (pharmacies, hospitals, etc.); pros and cons of different types of 
transportation in the neighbourhood and city'' means, but may not know how to say these things in another 
language if this has not yet been covered in their existing curriculum. However, this existing knowledge in 
their own language will help them recognise, understand, and learn the new information more easily in a 
foreign language, in this case English.
Key Concepts
Life in the neighbourhood, life in the city, social life, attractions and recreational places (cafes, cinemas; 
gyms, sports clubs), services in the neighbourhood and city (pharmacies, hospitals, etc.), pros and cons of 
different types of transportation in the neighbourhood and city.
INDICATORS FOR LEARNING (Assessment and Evaluation) 
PERFORMANCE ASSIGNMENT: Students conduct an interview with a person from their neighbourhood 
such as a driver, a chemist, a neighbour, an officer etc. about past experiences of these people in the city in 
terms of public transportation, the pros and cons of different types of transportation in the neighbourhood 
and city, getting help from services in the neighbourhood and the city and social life. Students begin by 
preparing interview questions. Next, they elicit and decide the questions. Then, they choose the person 
for the interview. They may use avatars or AI to create characters for the interviews. The interview should 
include creative elements, such as charts, or videos, and students will present their findings in class. The 
assignment will be evaluated by using a rubric scheme, a rating scale, and/or a marking scheme.
