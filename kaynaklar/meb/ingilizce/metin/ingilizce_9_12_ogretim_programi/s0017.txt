THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE CURRICULUM
17
• In English Language Curriculum, one of the three “Supportive Skills”, “Vocabulary: Selection-Use Skill” 
is based on the learner’s positioning and using the target words after recognising, perceiving, and 
understanding them through “Receptive Skills” in the target content in a way that is appropriate for the 
context, situation, place and time. They also remember, recall and add previously learned vocabulary 
that are appropriate for the situation and context, to the context, and select and use them correctly, 
effectively and meaningfully. These stages have been identified, revealed and applied in the “process 
components” of the “field skills” and then transferred to the “learning outcomes”, and finally to the 
“learning-teaching experiences” sections of the themes in The English Language Curriculum.
• “Vocabulary acquisition” is the situation in which children pick up and adopt words without noticing and 
analysing them in their mother tongue. “Vocabulary learning” on the other hand, is the process that takes 
place in the artificial environment of the classroom, not in the natural contexts and environments of 
second/foreign language. In order to be sure that words are used naturally, authentically, correctly, 
meaningfully, and effectively in learners’ interaction and communication processes, it is necessary to 
practise them by being aware of their meaning in a way that is appropriate to the context, their form, 
pronunciation and uses. Activities in which this skill is used should be carefully planned, designed, and 
created in a contextual and meaningful way in the lesson planning and implementation process included 
in The English Language Curriculum so that learners can use English vocabulary in a natural, authentic, 
automatic, realistic, effective, efficient and meaningful way in context and thus gain communicative 
competence.
• The third of the three “Supportive Skills” in The English Language Curriculum is the “Pronunciation: 
Selection and Use Skill”. Phonological skills help learners to use the sounds, accents, rhythms, tones, 
intonation, and pronunciation of the language correctly; to reflect the content of the speech correctly; 
and to interact and communicate efficiently and effectively. This skill is supported with activities through 
listening practice including listening exercises, repetition, imitation, pronunciation exercises, listening 
to peer recordings and giving individual feedback, listening to individual voice recordings and reflecting 
individually during automatic selection, use and reflection sessions. As with other skills, the correct and 
appropriate use of pronunciation facilitates learners’ language learning experience by giving meaning to 
the content. This content should support the provided verbal information and make the content of the 
verbal information meaningful. In lesson planning and implementation, activities that use this skill need 
to be planned, designed, and built in a contextual and meaningful way where learners are expected to 
use English pronunciation naturally, authentically, efficiently, automatically, and meaningfully.
• The English Language Curriculum also aims to support learners to develop their “sociolinguistic and 
pragmatic competence” through communicative and collaborative activities relevant to different 
cultural content and contexts. The “social language expressions” used in all the themes are part of 
this, as learners can access the real- world language by learning them inside the classroom and using 
them inside or outside the classroom in their personal lives. For supporting learners to acquire “social 
competence” through meaningful oral/verbal and in written contexts in a way that is appropriate to their 
English language levels, it is necessary to provide opportunities for them to use English effectively, 
meaningfully correctly, naturally, authentically, realistically, and fluently in their social communication, 
inside and outside school. This need to be considered in the design and implementation of all course 
contents very carefully.
• As described in The English Language Curriculum, it is essential that all processes written for “learning 
outcomes” and “process components” need to be considered and implemented in the lessons. In addition 
to this, in recommended practice, lesson planning activities should be carried out counting all related 
and integrated programme components, especially the “learning outcomes and process components” 
of the relevant theme. All the applications can be differentiated accordingly.
• In the English language classroom, it is necessary to create a learning environment that encourages 
the active participation of learners. Similarly, a classroom climate in which personal ideas can be shared 
independently and the development of social and emotional skills is supported should be created.
