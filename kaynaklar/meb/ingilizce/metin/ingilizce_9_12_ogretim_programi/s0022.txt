4. Evaluating vocabulary recording: Objective: To create personalised paper or digital dictionaries 
with word-related information, including definitions, antonyms, synonyms, word structure, etc. 
about the target vocabulary in context. Indicators: Regular entries with accurate definitions, 
antonyms, synonyms, relevance to the target content, organisation, meaningful content, continuity 
and order. Frequency: Every two months on a regular basis. Evaluation/Assessment tools: Oral 
and/or written feedback and a rubric scheme given to students in advance. Evaluation/Assessment 
focus: Evaluation is based on the categories such as accuracy, creativity, effort, completeness and 
organisation of the entries/submissions.
5. Evaluating the use of target grammar: Objective: Selecting and using the target grammar 
accurately and meaningfully in context. Indicators: Accurate and meaningful sentence structures 
in speaking and writing tasks, accurate and meaningful use of grammar in communicative activities. 
Frequency: During and after observation and monitoring on a regular basis. Evaluation/Assessment 
tools: A checklist, rating scale, and/or rubrics. Evaluation/Assessment focus: Evaluation is based 
on categories and criteria created in evaluation tools as in the following examples: An example of 
a checklist could be as follows: a) Sentences are complete: YES /NO. b) Word order is logical and 
clear: Yes/No, etc. An example of a rating scale with a 1-5 Likert scale could be as follows: (1) Errors 
interfere with meaning (2) Frequent errors, meaning is unclear (3) Errors are noticeable but do not 
interfere with meaning, (4) Minor errors, meaning is clear (5) Almost no errors. (An example grammar 
rubric table might include the following criteria and scores: 5 criteria could be used: accuracy, 
variety, consistency, structural control, appropriateness in the rubrics. They can be scored on 4 
levels: (1) poor, (2) needs improvement, (3) good, (4) excellent. For instance, there may be items such 
as (1) inconsistent use of grammar, (2) inconsistencies distract from clarity, (3) few inconsistencies 
in grammar use, (4) grammar is consistent throughout the task).
6. Evaluating reading and comprehension skills: Objective: To read efficiently for comprehension by 
using various strategies such as reading aloud, speed reading, silent reading, and jigsaw reading. 
Indicators: Accurate understanding of texts and context, successful participation in various reading 
activities, responses to closed (display), semi-closed and open-ended questions in comprehension 
activities, increasing performance in paraphrasing, summarising, interpreting, deducing and critical 
thinking skills. Frequency: At least once in a month, on a regular basis. Evaluation/Assessment 
tools: An observation checklist and/monitoring grids, a rating scale, and/or rubrics, closed, semi-
closed and open-ended/communicative asking-answering exercises and activities, paraphrasing, 
summarising and deducing sessions. Evaluation/Assessment focus: Evaluation will be based 
on reading fluency, effective comprehension, ability/capability to infer meaning from the text, to 
interpret text, and to generate critical thinking from the text.
7. Evaluating speaking skills: Objective: To talk or give oral presentations on the current content by 
using meaningful and appropriate language in context. Indicators: Effective use of relevant and 
appropriate target vocabulary, pronunciation and target grammar; fluency; clear organisation; 
rapport with the audience and delivery. Frequency: During observation of presentation sessions. 
Evaluation/Assessment tools: Rubrics, a rating scale, and/or a checklist for observation and 
monitoring. Evaluation/Assessment focus: Evaluation will be based on fluency, coherence, 
accurate and meaningful use of language with target vocabulary, pronunciation, and grammar in 
context, audience engagement and successful participation in communication by encoding and 
decoding the messages accurately and meaningfully.
8. Evaluating participation and engagement in dialogues: Objectives: To take turns in dialogues 
meaningfully and efficiently by using relevant and appropriate target vocabulary and grammar in 
context. Indicators: Proper and accurate use of language with target vocabulary and grammar 
in context, effective turn-taking, engagement and participation in teacher-student and student-
peer dialogues, correct and meaningful responses, clear explanations, engaging in effective 
and meaningful communication. Frequency: Regularly, during classroom activities. Evaluation/ 
Assessment tools: An observation checklist and/or rubrics for observation and monitoring.
