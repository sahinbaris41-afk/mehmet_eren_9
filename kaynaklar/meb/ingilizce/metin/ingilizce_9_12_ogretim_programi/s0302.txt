Generalisations/Key Concepts  
Generalisations
The target concept of this theme ''family life and home with its components such as family members and their 
daily routines today and in the past; hobbies and interests of family members (sports & films); different types 
of houses and families from the world'' is present in almost all languages. Students who start to learn a new 
language inevitably bring with them their pre-existing knowledge and transfer it to their new language learning 
context. Most example, a student in an upper secondary school may know what ''family life and home with its 
components such as family members and their daily routines today and in the past; hobbies and interests of 
family members (sports & films); different types of houses and families from the world'' means, but may not 
know how to say these things in a foreign language if this has not yet been covered in their existing curriculum. 
However, this existing knowledge in their own language will help them recognise, understand, and learn the 
new information more easily in a foreign language, in this case English.

INDICATORS FOR LEARNING (Assessment and Evaluation) 
PERFORMANCE ASSIGNMENT: Students will create a project titled "Family Life and Home," exploring the 
dynamics of family life across time and cultures. They will begin by describing the daily routines of their 
family members, comparing these routines to those of previous generations to highlight how roles and 
responsibilities have evolved. Next, they will focus on hobbies and interests within the family, such as 
favourite sports or films, and explain how these activities contribute to relations and personal expression. 
Finally, students will research different types of houses and family structures around the world, comparing 
them to their own experiences and reflecting on how cultural differences influence family life and home 
environments. The project should include creative elements such as family interviews, photos, drawings, 
or videos and will be presented in class. The assignment will be evaluated by using a rubric scheme, a rating 
scale, and/or a marking scheme.
