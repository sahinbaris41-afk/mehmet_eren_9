Activity 2: Paired Picture Comparison - Living Room Activities
The teacher provides each pair of students with two different pictures of the same room (e.g., a living room). 
In one picture, there might be a father watching TV, while in the other, a mother might be reading a newspaper 
in the same room. Each student receives one picture and does not show it to their peer. The students take 
turns asking questions to discover what is happening in their peer’s picture. For example, Student A might 
ask, ''Is anyone sitting on the sofa?'' or ''What is the person in your picture doing?'' This way, they work together 
to fill in the missing information about their own pictures. Once the activity is complete, both pictures are 
displayed on the board, and the class compares them to see the similarities and differences between 
the two. The teacher and the class provide feedback on the meaning, accuracy and completeness of the 
descriptions, as well as the students’ use of vocabulary and grammar. Students reflect on their thoughts and 
feelings about the activity.

Activity 1: House and Activity Pictionary
In this activity, the teacher prepares small pieces of paper with vocabulary related to rooms, furniture, and 
activities in the house (e.g., bedroom, dining table, brushing teeth, ironing clothes), folds them, and places 
them in a box. The class is divided into teams, and one student from each team picks a piece of paper and 
must draw the word on the board without speaking. The other team members guess what is being drawn, 
and once they guess correctly, they must use the word in a sentence that describes an action at home (e.g., 
''My sister is watching TV in the living room.''). The teacher awards points for correct guesses and accurately 
formed sentences, and the team with the highest score wins. Students reflect on their thoughts and feelings 
about the activity.
Objective: To describe different rooms, furniture, and activities in the house in English by using target 
vocabulary and target grammar.
Activity 2: House Vocabulary Bingo
The teacher gives students a bingo card with rooms, furniture, and activities. S/he calls out the words, and 
students mark the words they hear on their cards. The first student to complete a bingo wins and forms 
sentences using the words from their bingo card and says it aloud. Then, the teacher evaluates the student’s 
sentences for meaning, accuracy, correct use of vocabulary, and grammar. Students reflect on their 
thoughts and feelings about the activity.
