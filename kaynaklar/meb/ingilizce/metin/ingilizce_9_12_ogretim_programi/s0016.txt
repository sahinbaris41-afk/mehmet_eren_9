• Grammar “acquisition” is the situation in which children naturally pick up, adopt, and use the grammatical 
items and forms (and other elements of the language) of their mother tongue, in a realistic and natural 
environment and context, without conscious focus, without being aware of it, in an automatic, natural 
and real way. In the case of foreign language education provided at school, it is described as “language 
learning” (including grammar and other elements of the language). The elements that define language 
learning are the artificial environment, planning, conscious learning of rules and short-term goals. In 
The English Language Curriculum, teaching grammaring aims to build an environment where learners 
can communicate by using the language with correct grammar in a natural, realistic and meaningful 
way in context and on a correct and appropriate methodological basis that can make this possible in a 
natural, realistic, automatic, and meaningful way. This can only be achieved to a certain extent, and the 
necessary condition for this, is building a continuous, natural and authentic environment in the classroom 
where grammar is not taught as a subject by the teacher but through the use of appropriate methods, 
techniques, materials, and activities. For this reason, the process should be supported by a plenty of 
natural and realistic repetition, revision, interaction, storytelling, role-playing, and communication 
activities. In The English Language Curriculum , the grammar learning process of primary school pupils 
(Years 2, 3, 4) is described with the “Holistic Approach”, where the functions of the right hemisphere of 
the brain are found to be more effective, as explained in the relevant literature, helping them see, hear, 
and use grammar automatically without being aware of its rules, and where features and activities such 
as visuals, rhythm, music, movement, drama, and storytelling are used through all aspects affecting 
learning (physical, cognitive, affective, cultural, and social) together. The process of learning grammar 
in Years 5 and 6 of lower secondary school is described as the “Semi-Holistic/Semi-Inductive Approach”, 
using features and activities such as visuals, rhythm, music, movement, drama, and storytelling, which 
still allow the functions of the right hemisphere of the brain to be effective. At the same time, the 
functions of the left hemisphere of the brain, which provides awareness, natural and simple inferences, 
and very simple discoveries, are gradually activated, but there is a condition that grammar should not be 
taught as a subject by the teacher. Noticing and awareness activities are the most important activities 
of this approach and do not involve teacher explanation or conscious discovery tasks. For learners in 
Years 7, 8 of lower secondary school, Years 9, 10, 11, 12 and Year Prep and After Prep-Years 9, 10, 11, 12 
of upper secondary school the “Inductive Approach” is described to learn grammar in a meaningful and 
appropriate context by discovering the rules themselves. The functions of the left hemisphere of the 
brain are more effective in this discovery process, by being aware of their meaning, function, usage, 
form, by using higher-level thinking skills such as examining, comparing and classifying, and by making 
inferences based on their meaning without using terminology or formulations. In this approach, the 
functions of the right hemisphere of the brain can still be activated through the activities such as visuals, 
music, movement, drama, and storytelling in the created course content. In the learning process, it is 
necessary to create continuous, natural, authentic, and realistic contexts and environments where 
grammar is not taught as a subject by the teacher in the school classroom but is facilitated to help 
learners use the language by selecting and using grammar with appropriate techniques and activities 
in these natural and realistic contexts. In The English Language Curriculum , activating the functions of  
both hemispheres of the brain as much as possible in all lessons is encouraged so all the activities need 
to be carefully planned, designed and created in a contextual and meaningful way in lesson planning 
and practice so that learners can and use English grammar in a natural, authentic, automatic, realistic, 
effective, efficient, and meaningful way according to the context, and thus can acquire and develop their 
communicative competence.
