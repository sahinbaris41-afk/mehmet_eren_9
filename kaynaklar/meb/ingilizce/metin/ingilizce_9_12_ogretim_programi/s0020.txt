• In The English Language Curriculum “values” are presented according to the target content in the 
theme, the process of learning-teaching experiences, and learning outcomes. Learners are expected to 
internalise these values and reflect them in their daily lives. For this, learners needed to be provided with 
the opportunity to apply the value they realise during the process of examining and understanding the 
content in the activities in the themes.
• In the studies conducted in The English Language Curriculum or in the products such as projects, 
portfolios, poems, songs, articles, stories, reviews, etc., attention should be paid to issues related to 
copyright and protection of personal data. Content should be created that complies with the constitution 
and laws, considers the sensitivities of individuals or society, reflects national and moral values, and 
respects the rights and freedoms of individuals. Learners should be made aware of their legal rights and 
responsibilities when creating and sharing content.
• In The English Language Curriculum, the activities included in the expansion and supporting sections 
in the “differentiation” section do not take place in teaching materials. However, some e-contents are 
prepared by the material developers for the implementation of their contents. All practices and activities 
within the scope of differentiation should be planned and implemented by teachers, taking into account 
the interests, needs, and requests of the learners.
• It is important to include reading texts that broaden students’ horizons, inspire and encourage them, 
and reflect Türkiye’s achievements to date, its internationally renowned works, its visibility, vision, and 
recent successes, as well as texts, including digital content, that explain the significant and unique 
contributions of our country’s institutions and people in The English Language Curriculum. It is essential 
that the selected reading texts align with national, spiritual, cultural, and moral values of our country. 
Reading materials should be chosen to support the idea that students learn about the values of our 
history, culture, and civilisation accurately.
• In The English Language Curriculum, the aims, objectives, methods, criteria and tools of assessment/
evaluation based on the learners’ English levels have been included as an essential part. An alternative 
assessment/evaluation scheme has been designed to assess/evaluate English-related knowledge 
and skills in creating new meaningful content and communicating through it. The components of The 
English Language Curriculum have been made a meaningful part of learning and evaluation. Instead of 
assessing/evaluating “values, dispositions, literacy skills and social-emotional learning skills” with marks 
and scores, some alternative evaluation tools including classroom-based and/or out-of-classroom 
activities such as performance tasks, portfolios, projects, diaries, interviews, drama studies, etc. have 
been used to evaluate their development together. These components are also among the criteria 
taken into consideration in the evaluation tools and marking/scoring keys of such performances and 
achievements. These alternative evaluation techniques have been included in The English Language 
Curriculum to evaluate language skills, collaborative activities such as pair and group work, and creative, 
productive, and communicative activities according to language levels. The evaluation criteria for each 
skill are listed as “purposes, indicators, application, frequency of use, evaluation tools, and evaluation
