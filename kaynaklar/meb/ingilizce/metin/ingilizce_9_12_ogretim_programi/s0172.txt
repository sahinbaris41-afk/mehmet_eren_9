Activity 2: Festival Information Gap-Fill Listening Activity
The teacher prepares short audio clips describing different festivals and foods (e.g., ''In Japan, the Ramen 
Festival offers a variety of noodle dishes. Chefs prepare bowls of ramen with broth, vegetables, and chicken, 
and egg toppings'') and upload them to a web tool to create a listening gap-fill exercise. Students listen to 
the audio clips and complete the missing information directly on the web tool (e.g., ''The ___ Festival offers a 
variety of noodle dishes''). They then record themselves using an audio recording tool, repeating the sentences 
fluently. The teacher plays some of the recordings in class and provides feedback on pronunciation and 
sentence accuracy, while students also give peer feedback to improve their speaking skills. Students reflect 
on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.

Objective: To describe different kinds of food produced for local and international festivals and food culture

Activity 1: Festival Food Matching Game
The teacher gives students cards in two different categories: food items in the first category and festivals in 
the second. Students work in pairs, and each pair receives the cards. They match each food with the relevant 
festival. (e.g., Adana Orange Flower Festival – orange jam) After completing the matching, students create 
simple sentences based on examples shown on the board, such as: ''This is a local festival. They make orange 
jam at the Adana Orange Flower Festival''. The teacher encourages students to say their sentences aloud, 
helping them practise pronunciation. The pair that completes the correct matches the fastest and creates 
accurate sentences wins the activity. Students reflect on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
Objective: To help students recognise and identify local and international festivals and food culture in English 
by using target vocabulary and target grammar.
Activity 2: Festival Food Listening Activity
The teacher prepares short audio clips describing different festivals and food (e.g., ''In France, at the Omelette 
Festival, they cook a large omelette in a big pan'') and uploads them to a web tool to create a listening quiz. 
Students listen to the audio clips and select the correct answer from multiple-choice options with the 
corresponding festival or food item (e.g., Omelette Festival – Omelette). After completing the listening part, 
students work in pairs to practise the sentences from the audio and record themselves using an audio 
recording tool, repeating the target sentences. The teacher plays some of the recordings and provides 
feedback on pronunciation and sentence accuracy, while students also give peer feedback. Students reflect 
on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
