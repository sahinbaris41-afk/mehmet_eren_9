RECONSTRUCTING/COMMUNICATING THROUGH SPEAKING: Collaborative and communicative activities are 
designed and used to revise and consolidate the whole theme on “school life and education with comparing 
different types of schools; comparing different education systems; national days and celebrations” (V4.5, 
LS5, CS2.13, CS2.14). Storification, drama, and gamification techniques are actively and extensively used 
for further speaking practice throughout the theme (SELS2.1, SELS2.2, V4.1, V4.2, LS4, LS9). Students 
are encouraged to produce several spoken products related to the extended contexts such as dialogues, 
speeches, short texts, presentations, etc. to interact and to communicate with peers and/or other people 
inside and/or outside the school by using the language including the target words, language chunks, and 
social language expressions for the current theme (SELS2.3, V3.4, V7.1, V7.2, LS1, CS2.20). AI-based 
technologies can be used for various activities, giving students access to authentic materials. They can 
also produce digital materials and projects for speaking at school and at home through recordings (D3.3, 
SELS3.1, SELS3.2, V7.3, V11.1, LS2). All these speaking activities can be evaluated as described in items (1), 
(2), (3), (4), (5), (7), (8), (9), (10), and (11) in the section “1.2. Principles for the Implementation of the English

REFLECTING THROUGH SPEAKING: Students are encouraged to reflect individually or collaboratively in 
groups on their own individual learning (SELS1.3) or on their collaborative learning in pairs, groups or as a 
whole class collaboratively (D2.3, SELS2.2, CS2.15). The activities will help and support students in these 
feedback and reflection sessions. Their feedback is shared with peers and the teacher and takes the form 
of interactive and ongoing feedback that can help their own improvement (SELS1.2, SELS2.1) and also they 
contribute to the improvement of their peers in their learning processes (V4.1, V4.3, V12.1, V12.3, LS1).
DIFFERENTIATION
Expansion
Objective: To talk about different types of schools and different education systems in English through a 
discussion activity by using target vocabulary and target grammar.
Activity 1: Exploring School Systems through Group Discussions
The teacher informs students that they work in small groups to discuss different types of schools and 
different education systems topics. The teacher prepares discussion topics, such as ''What makes a school 
modern and well-equipped?'' and ''How can schools provide equal opportunities for all students?'' writes them 
on small cards, and places them in a box. Each group sends one student to draw a card from the box. The 
group discusses the chosen question, making sure that each member contributes by using target vocabulary 
(e.g., diverse, challenging, collaborative) and target grammar structures such as modal verbs of deduction 
(e.g., The library must be an important facility for modern schools) and passive voice (e.g., Opportunities are 
provided to students in student-centred schools). After the discussion, the group writes their key points and 
conclusions, then reads them aloud to the class. The teacher listens carefully and provides constructive 
feedback on target vocabulary usage, grammar accuracy, and pronunciation. Students reflect on their 
thoughts and feelings about the activity.
