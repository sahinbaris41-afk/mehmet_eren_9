REFLECTING THROUGH SPEAKING: Students are encouraged to reflect individually or collaboratively in 
groups on their own individual learning (SELS1.3) or on their collaborative learning in pairs, groups or as a 
whole class collaboratively (D2.3, SELS2.2, CS2.15). The activities will help and support students in these

Expansion
Objective: To talk about activities, services, and transportation options in the city and neighbourhood by 
listing pros and cons in English by using target vocabulary and target grammar.
Activity 1: City Life Discussions
The teacher divides the class into three groups with specific topics:
Group 1: Activities in attractions and recreational places (e.g., cafés, cinemas, gyms).
Group 2: Getting help from services in the neighbourhood and city (e.g., pharmacies, hospitals).
Group 3: Pros and cons of different types of transportation in the neighbourhood and city.
Each group creates a list of pros and cons using the target vocabulary (e.g., ''Public transport saves time but 
is often crowded'', or ''Chatting with friends at a café feels relaxing but can be expensive''). Groups present 
their ideas to the class, and other students ask questions such as, ''Why do you think it is reliable?'' or ''Isn’t it 
time-consuming?'' After the discussion, each student writes a short paragraph and explains if s/he agrees or 
disagrees with one idea and why. The teacher provides feedback on accuracy, use of target vocabulary, and 
pronunciation. Students reflect on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.

Objective: To describe past activities in different places in English by using target vocabulary and target 
grammar.
Activity 1: What Were We Doing?
The teacher writes some target words on the board for different places:
At the café: drinking tea, chatting with friends.
At the gym: lifting weights, doing pilates.
At the cinema: watching a movie, eating popcorn.
The teacher gives an example and writes it on the board:
''I was drinking tea at the cafe''.
Then, students come to the board one by one and write their own sentences (e.g., ''We were watching a movie 
at the cinema''.).
Next, the teacher writes a question and answer on the board:
''What were you doing at the cafe?''
''I was chatting with friends''.
