 Functions of the Grammatical Structures in Use:
-Use of the Simple Present Tense: Describing a general state or habit for revision.
-Use of the Present Progressive Tense: Describing an action happening now or temporarily for revision.
-Use of ''Be going to'' future tense: Describing planned actions, decisions or predictions based on evidence 
for future revision.
-Use of the Simple Future Tense: Describing spontaneous decisions, promises, or predictions based on 
opinions for future revision.
-Use of Reported Speech of the Present and Future Tenses: Reporting actions, ideas or plans for present 
and future tenses.
-Use of verbs for reported speech: Ask, report, say, tell.

Generalisations/Key Concepts  
Generalisations
The target concept of this theme ''school life, classroom life and education with its components such as 
challenges and coping with challenges for success; graduation and future plans after school'' is present 
in almost all languages. Students who start to learn a new language inevitably bring with them their pre-
existing knowledge and transfer it to their new language learning context. For example, a student in an upper 
secondary school may know what ''school life, classroom life and education with its components such as 
challenges and coping with challenges for success; graduation and future plans after school'' means, but 
may not know how to say these things in a foreign language if this has not yet been covered in their existing 
curriculum. However, this existing knowledge in their own language will help them recognise, understand, 
and learn the new information more easily in a foreign language, in this case English.
Key Concepts
School life, classroom life, education, challenges, coping with challenges, success; graduation, future plans.
INDICATORS FOR LEARNING (Assessment and Evaluation) 
PERFORMANCE ASSIGNMENT: Students will study on the concept of ''School Life, Classroom Life, and 
Education'', focusing on the sub-themes of challenges and coping with challenges for success, graduation 
and future plans after school, and national days and celebrations. They will create a multimedia project, 
such as a video diary or a digital book, documenting a fictional student's journey through school. This journey 
should include their struggles with academic and social challenges, strategies they use to manage stress, 
and their plans and aspirations for life after graduation. Students will also incorporate reflections on how 
their school community celebrates national days and traditions, highlighting the role these events play in 
fostering unity and pride. The project should creatively show the emotional highs and lows of school life, 
the importance of setting goals, and the value of celebrating milestones and cultural traditions, providing a 
relatable and inspiring narrative. The assignment will be evaluated by using a rubric scheme, a rating scale, 
and/or a marking scheme.
