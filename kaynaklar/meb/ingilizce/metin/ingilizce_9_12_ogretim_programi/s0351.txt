Functions of the Target Grammatical Structures in Use:
-Use of the Present Perfect Tense: Describing actions that began in the past and continue into the present 
and describing an action that happened recently and has an impact on the present.
-Use of the “Question Tags”: Confirming information, encouraging agreement, seeking reassurance.
-Use of Countable Nouns and Uncountable Nouns: Describing an unspecified amount and uncertainty or 
absence, and expressing large quantities.
-Use of Modals “Need, Needn’t” for obligation: Describing that something is required or necessary or is not 
necessary or obligatory.

Generalisations/Key Concepts  
 Generalisations
The target concept of this theme ''continents, countries, and nationalities; shopping for food and personal 
food preferences; food and food culture from different parts of the world'' is present in almost all languages. 
Students who start to learn a new language inevitably bring with them their pre-existing knowledge and 
transfer it to their new language learning context. For example, a student in an upper secondary school may 
know what ''continents, countries, and nationalities; shopping for food and personal food preferences; food 
and food culture from different parts of the world'' mean but may not know how to say these things in another 
language if this has not yet been covered in their existing curriculum. However, this existing knowledge in 
their own language will help them recognise, understand, and learn the new information more easily in a 
foreign language, in this case English.
Key Concepts
Continents, countries, nationalities, shopping for food, personal food preferences, food and food culture 
from different parts of the world.
INDICATORS FOR LEARNING (Assessment and Evaluation) 
PERFORMANCE ASSIGNMENT: Students prepare a project titled "Global Food Culture and Preferences", 
focusing on the themes of continents, countries, and nationalities; shopping for food and personal food 
preferences; and food culture from different parts of the world. First, they research continents, countries, 
and nationalities, describing their unique features. Then, they explore how people shop for food, talk about 
personal preferences like vegan, halal, or gluten-free, and highlight traditional and modern dishes. Finally, 
they look at foods from different cultures, such as sushi, baklava, and pizza, and discuss healthy and unhealthy 
eating habits. They should use creative elements such as maps, charts, or videos and present their projects 
in class. The assignment will be evaluated by using a rubric scheme, a rating scale, and/or a marking scheme.
