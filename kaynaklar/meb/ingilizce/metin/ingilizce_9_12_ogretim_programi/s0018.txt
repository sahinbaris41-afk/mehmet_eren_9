THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE CURRICULUM
18
• Activities such as questioning, researching, observing and experimenting should be carried out with an 
interdisciplinary and contextual approach, based on the language level of learners in the class. They 
should be planned and implemented by the teachers of English departments carefully.
• In The English Language Curriculum, “establishing links” section has been described and designed as an 
important part before moving on to the “learning-teaching experiences” section of the themes. The first 
stage of the three-stages of “establishing links”, should provide the necessary preliminary information 
to facilitate the processing of the theme. The second stage should summarise what needs to be done 
to be ready to process the “learning-teaching practices” section. In the last stage, it should be indicated 
how the learners can use the knowledge, experience, and skills they develop throughout the themes, or 
to which disciplines they can transfer them and especially in which areas they can use them in their real 
lives.
• The “learning-teaching experiences” section in the themes is presented in a comprehensive framework 
to provide learners with a holistic perspective. This holistic perspective is seen as a supportive 
element for learners. The section guides the teacher to use different teaching approaches, methods, 
techniques, and activities to facilitate learners to become aware of their own learning and individual 
learning strategies they can use, to achieve constant learning, to perceive their own learning process, to 
be aware of the spiral flow and interdisciplinary relationships in the learning process within this holistic 
perspective along with the social use of the language in social contexts.
• “Reflection” is a significant component of The English Language Curriculum. Learning-teaching 
experiences are organised in a way that “field and supportive skills” and “learning outcomes” gain meaning 
together within the course flow and facilitate learners to reflect on their learning about the activities. 
Both individual and collaborative reflection takes place in the flow of “learning-teaching” process as 
described in the “field skills” and “learning outcomes” sections.
• The English Language Curriculum  has been prepared by taking into consideration “field skills and 
supportive skills”, “conceptual skills”, “dispositions”, “inter-curricular components (social-emotional 
learning skills, values, literacy skills)”, “inter-disciplinary relationships”, “inter-skills”, “content frame”, 
“generalisations and key concepts”, “indicators for learning”, “learning-teaching experiences (basic 
acceptations, pre-evaluation process, establishing links, learning-teaching practices)”, “differentiation 
(expansion and supporting)”, and “teacher reflections”. Therefore, learning outcomes should be 
addressed in accordance with the principles of progression, spirality, and sequentiality as specified in 
the themes.
• In the process and flow of “learning-teaching experiences” section, all language skills have been 
followed from easy to difficult, from known to unknown, from more controlled to more open- 
ended and communicative, according to the relevant language level. Branching learning has been 
attempted throughout these processes. Collaborative, interactive, and communicative activities 
have been suggested throughout the themes constantly. The main aim of foreign language learning, 
“to communicate by filling information gaps through reciprocal and mutual interaction, sharing, and 
exchanging ideas”, has been included in the flow of “learning-teaching experiences” section by using all 
language skills. This flow and methodological stance should be followed in the classroom to facilitate 
learners’ learning experiences.
• One of the principal aspects of The English Language Curriculum is field and supportive skills which 
are pursued, processed, and developed in a holistic, integrated, spiral and recycling manner within the 
themes. To provide and maintain this, each theme begins with a holistic introductory story, as a context 
for teaching and learning the language. The introductory story is planned to be a digital film with a script 
presenting the linguistic and semantic elements such as target grammar, vocabulary, phonological 
aspects (pronunciation, intonation, etc.), and social language expressions, appropriate to the language 
level, needed to learn the theme. The perception of the general topic of the theme is provided with 
the visuality and movement of the digital introductory story (film). The target grammatical structures,
