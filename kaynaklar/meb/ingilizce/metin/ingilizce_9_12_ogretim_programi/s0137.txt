PERFORMANCE ASSIGNMENT: Students prepare a self-recording (visual, audio) at school/ at home, conducting

LEARNING-TEACHING EXPERIENCES
Basic Acceptations: Preparedness/Readiness
It is generally accepted that people who start to learn a new language bring with them their pre-existing 
knowledge and transfer it to their new language learning. For instance, this theme is based on the concept of  
''the types of houses, rooms, furniture, and activities in the house'' and  these students are expected to know 
what a house is, what a room is, what a piece of furniture is, and what an activity to do in the house is in their 
own language, as they are in year 9 (nine) of upper-secondary school. They are also aware that most people 
live in different types of houses with different rooms and furniture, and that they do different activities in their 
houses. Students can think about and express such facts and situations in their own language, but they may 
not know how to say them in a foreign language if they have not been covered at all or only partially covered 
in their existing curriculum. However, this existing knowledge in their own language would prepare them to 
identify the same concepts later and help them recognise, understand, and learn the new information about 
the new concept and theme more easily in their foreign language, English.
In terms of preparation for the current new theme, it is assumed that the students have learnt some basic 
vocabulary items for ''the types of houses, rooms, furniture, and activities in the house'' such as ''house, living 
room, bedroom, kitchen, sofa, bed, chair, to wash, to sleep, to eat etc.'' in previous themes or years. It is also 
assumed that they have learnt how to ask and answer questions to introduce houses, rooms, furniture, and 
activities such as ''How many rooms are there in your house? There are 3 rooms in our house. Is your house big? 
No, It’s/is small'' through grammatical structures that practise ''verb to be (am/is/are) in the Simple Present 
Tense in English from the previous themes or years. These vocabulary items and grammatical structures 
will be used as a base by revising and also extending them to learn the new or additional vocabulary and 
grammatical structures in the current theme by making the new grammar elements easier to learn.
Pre-Evaluation Process 
It is expected that students’ physical, cognitive, affective, social, and cultural factors are appropriate and 
suitable for learning about ''the types of houses, rooms, furniture, and activities in the house'' as a topic. 
Recalling and practising basic vocabulary for ''the types of houses, rooms, furniture, and activities in the 
house'' in English that they have learnt in the previous years would be helpful as a basis for moving on and 
learning the current content on the concept of ''the types of houses, rooms, furniture, and activities in the 
house''. Therefore, a quiz game can be played where each student can ask a simple question about the types 
of their houses, the rooms, furniture, and activities in their houses using the words s/he already knows (learnt 
in the previous years and revised at the beginning of the year) in pairs or in larger groups to revise all these 
vocabulary and grammatical structures from the previous themes or previous years by giving instructions 
in English as a preparation practice. In this way, their performance can be evaluated to see how much they 
remember and how much they are able to understand, select and use the previously learnt vocabulary and 
grammar to build their competence in the current theme. It can be decided what to emphasise or practise 
in order to revise the previous knowledge and to facilitate the recognition, selection and use of target 
vocabulary and grammatical items in the current theme.
