• Field skills are used as the basis for creating the learning outcomes in the English Language Curriculum. 
Process components are identified for each field skill, as well as for each learning outcome. These 
process components are the basic elements that provide access to the learning outcomes. Accordingly, 
each process component needs to be experienced by learners in the English language classroom.
• The English Language Curriculum is structured on “foreign language field skills and supportive skills” 
included in the Common Document of the Century of Türkiye Education Model.
• In the English Language Curriculum, activities and applications have been designed by considering most 
appropriate approaches, methods, and techniques according to their ages, and language levels. In 
line with the Post-Method Era Approach to teaching English as a foreign language, the emphasis is on 
selecting the accurate, appropriate, suitable, and effective techniques and activities for the right place, 
time, context, material, situation and purpose, according to the age and level of the learners, rather than 
a specific and single teaching method. Factors that affect language acquisition and learning such as 
the learners’ age, learning styles, motivation levels, aptitudes, interests, and personality traits are given 
significant consideration. This strand should be considered and used in an accurate, meaningful and 
effective way in lesson planning and implementation. Appropriate techniques that can respond to learners’ 
ages, learning styles, aptitudes, language levels, areas of interest, motivation levels and communication 
needs and also physical conditions they are in, should be considered, and necessary activities should be 
designed and implemented accordingly. In this way, they can be supported to acquire and develop their 
language competence in English. The activities based on techniques from different methods should not 
be designed in an aimless and ordinary way but in a way that helps learners discover their own learning 
purposes, ways, situations, and desires. Opportunities needed to be provided for learners to reflect on 
their learning in this way and to improve their own learning through their own reflection. Individual and 
collaborative reflection has a great importance in the English Language Curriculum, and has taken place 
as the last component of each field skill and learning outcome. 
• The first of the two Receptive Skills in the English Language Curriculum is the “Listening/Watching-
Comprehension Skill”, through which an individual develops to perceive and make sense of the oral-
verbal language content produced by a speaker or other source. These sources include the structural 
features of the language such as the accent, stress, speed, rhythm and also include content and 
semantic features (related to the theme) such as idioms, terms, words, phrases, clauses, sentences 
and social and cultural reflections in the context. Learners perceive and understand these with the 
help of a variety of visuals while learning English during the noticing and recognition” stages. “Reading-
Comprehension Skill” is a skill that is developed to perceive and make sense of the written content (text) 
which is produced with the help of the text type, visual support, use of grammar, use of vocabulary, 
use of idiomatic language, and the use of all semantic features in this content. Similarly, reading is very 
important for students to ‘identify, notice, and interpret’ the content and semantic features (related to 
the theme) such as idioms, terms, words, expressions, phrases, sentences, and the social and cultural 
reflections in the context. The structural and semantic features of the language are of great importance 
for both reception and comprehension.
• “Listening/Watching-Comprehension Skill” is a real time, highly fluid and rapid process therefore requires 
fluid, instant reception and understanding and also rapid and meaningful response and feedback. 
“Reading-Comprehension”, on the other hand, is a more static, controlled and slower process. In both 
“receptive skills”, the content conveyed to the learner and learner’s perception of the meaning of the 
message are based on her/his personal and social relationship to that content. In the English Language
