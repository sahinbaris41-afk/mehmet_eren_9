Expansion
Objective: To talk about endangered animals and conservation efforts in English by using the target 
vocabulary and target grammar.
Activity 1: Digital Role-Play: Save an Endangered Animal
Students work in small groups and choose or are assigned an endangered animal (e.g., (Antalya) Caretta 
caretta turtles or Anatolian leopards). Using a web tool, they create a digital character or visual story that 
introduces the animal, its habitat, and the problems it faces (such as pollution or habitat loss). The animal 
''speaks'' using recorded audio or text-to-speech to describe its challenges and suggest solutions. Groups 
present their digital work to the class while others take notes and give feedback on vocabulary, fluency, 
and presentation skills. After all presentations, the class holds a brainstorming session to discuss ideas for 
protecting the animals, using phrases like ''Every little bit helps!'' and ''We need to act now!''. The teacher 
provides feedback on the presentations, and students finish with a clear plan of actions and solutions. 
Students reflect on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
Objective: To identify endangered animals and conservation efforts in English by using the target vocabulary 
and target grammar.
Activity 2: Collaborative Reading Puzzle: Endangered Animals
The teacher divides a complex reading text about endangered animals (e.g., caretta caretta turtles, Anatolian 
leopards) into sections, each with unique information (like habitat, threats, or solutions). Students work in 
small groups, and each group reads one section, identifying key points and preparing questions about missing 
information they need from other groups. After reading, groups take turns asking and answering questions 
to complete the full picture of the topic (e.g., ''What is threatening the Anatolian leopard?''). Once they gather 
all the information, each group presents a summary of their findings, combining what they read and learned 
from others. The class ends with a discussion on solutions to protect endangered animals. The teacher gives 
feedback on reading comprehension, vocabulary use, and collaboration throughout the activity. Students 
reflect on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
Supporting
Objective: To describe endangered animals, their habitats, and ways to protect them from extinction in 
English by using the target vocabulary and target grammar.
Activity 1: Endangered Animal Habitat Matching Game
The teacher provides students with two sets of cards: one set with endangered animals (e.g., polar bear, sea 
turtle) and another with habitats (e.g., ocean, rainforest). Students work in pairs to match the animals with 
their correct habitats (e.g., Polar bear-Arctic). Once matched, they create simple sentences following the 
models on the board, such as ''A polar bear lives in the ocean'' or ''A tiger lives in the jungle'', and practice saying 
them aloud to improve pronunciation. The pair that completes the matches and sentences correctly and the 
fastest wins the game. Students reflect on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
Objective: To identify endangered animals and their habitats in English by using target vocabulary and target 
grammar.
Activity 2: Endangered Animals Gap-Fill and Pronunciation Practice
The teacher starts by providing a short reading or audio about endangered animals like (Antalya) Caretta 
caretta sea turtles or Anatolian leopards. Students learn about their habitats, challenges, and ways to 
protect them. Afterward, students use the Web 2.00 tool to complete a gap-fill exercise with sentences such 
as ''A Caretta caretta turtle lays eggs on the (Mediterranean coast)''. After completing the exercise, students 
read their sentences aloud to practise pronunciation. The teacher then provides feedback on their accuracy 
and fluency.
The activity ends with a class discussion on protecting animals, using phrases like ''Every little bit helps!'' and 
''It’s not too late to act!''. Students reflect on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
