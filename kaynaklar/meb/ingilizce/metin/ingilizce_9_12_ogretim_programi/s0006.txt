Reducing the Number of Themes  
The English Language Curriculum has been simplified by considering the number of the weekly lessons by 
reducing the number of themes. It is organised in such a way so as to confirm that the themes are covered 
in greater depth and that all the skills activities are implemented effectively by allocating sufficient time. In 
addition, “consolidation, differentiation, and reflection” activities are integrated into this thematic structure. 
Since the number of themes has been reduced, the content of each theme might appear dense, however, 
this is due to the revisional and consolidating nature of the whole programme. Even though there are fewer 
themes and they may seem dense, they actually include less content compared to previous programmes. 
Simultaneous and Common Revision Programme
In the English Language Curriculum, a four or three-week revision programme at the beginning of each 
academic year has been planned and designed for all years and levels. This revision programme has been 
prepared by merging eight themes in each year into two themes to be taught at the beginning of the year. 
Lesson materials are to be designed for this situation for the revision of the previous level before each new 
level in the programme. Thus it is aimed to regulate all levels of English by providing that they are revised 
simultaneously and with the same content in each level in every part of our country. There is also an 
orientation programme for one or two weeks at the beginning of the school year to be applied in all schools 
nationwide.
The Unique Thematic Model  
The thematic sequence of the English Language Curriculum is based on eight main themes covering eight 
major concepts of life in all years and at all levels. Then, each main theme is divided into sub-themes which 
cover the main concepts in more detail and in smaller units. The sub-themes of the main themes are organised 
in a structure that complements each other but gradually deepens and becomes more comprehensive as 
years and levels progress. However, sub-topics change across all years and levels and are not the same in 
any year or level.  In this way, the aim is to cover all life-related subjects and concepts throughout learners’ 
language education and to equip them with the ability to understand, express and communicate their 
knowledge, thoughts, and feelings in English.
