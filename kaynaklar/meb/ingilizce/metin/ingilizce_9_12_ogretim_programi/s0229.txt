CONTENT FRAME  
SCHOOL LIFE & EDUCATION
Sub-themes: “Different types of schools in the world; regular and extracurricular activities in different types 
of schools; school anxiety and stress; national days and celebrations”
Target Vocabulary in Use (with revisional vocabulary):
Vocabulary for general terms related to education:
Nouns: An educational system, a diploma, a year group (a grade level), an extracurricular activity, a 
qualification. Verbs: To graduate, to succeed.
Vocabulary for types of schools: 
Nouns: A nursery, a preschool (kindergarten-AmE), a  primary school (elementary school- AmE), secondary 
education, a lower secondary school (junior high-middle school-AmE), an upper secondary school (senior 
high-highschool-AmE), a private school, a boarding school.
Vocabulary for facilities in different types of schools:
Nouns: A tennis / basketball court, a prayer room, a staffroom (teachers’ lounge-AmE),  a canteen (cafeteria, 
AmE), a courtyard, a locker room, food technology room (cooking lab-AmE)
Vocabulary for regular activities in different types of schools:  
Nouns: Classwork, a quiz, a presentation, laboratory work, literature, PE, religion and morals (RE).
Vocabulary for extracurricular activities in different types of schools: 
Nouns: Volleyball, gymnastics, rugby, badminton, photography, sculpture.
Vocabulary for clubs and competitions in different types of schools:
Nouns: A chess club, a literature club, an art club, a coding club, a volunteering club, a spelling bee club.
Vocabulary for school anxiety and stress: 
Nouns: Anxiety, time management.  Adjectives: Anxious, perfectionist.
National and religious days, festivals and celebrations: 
“29 October Republic Day”, “23 April National Sovereignty and Children’s Day”, “19 May Commemoration of 
Atatürk, Youth and Sports Day”, “15 July Democracy and National Unity Day”, “30 August Victory Day”, "Eid al-
Fitr (Eid-al-Ramadan)", "Festival of Sacrifice (Eid-al-Adha)". 
*(Relevant language content and (communicative) activities related to these national and religious days, 
festivals and celebrations will be developed and integrated across all themes and in all appropriate sections 
wherever and whenever possible.)
*Local Cultural, Social and  Moral Considerations of the Theme
In this theme, specific examples, cases, situations, events, and practices reflecting Turkish social, cultural, 
and moral customs, traditions, and heritage  will be included across all sub-topics. This consideration will be 
age-and-level appropriate and selected with the Turkish learners’ social and cultural context in mind.  A table 
of contents for this section is provided in the introduction to the whole English Language Curriculum. 
Target Grammatical Structures in Use:
-The Simple Present Tense vs The Present Progressive Tense: (The teacher explains the lesson every 
morning. The students are discussing a project in the seminar room right now.)
-Nothing, something, anything: (There is nothing in the locker room because the students cleared it out. The 
counsellor said there is something bothering the pupil, but they won’t talk about it. Is there anything you need 
help with before the exam?)
-Question tags for The Simple Present Tense & The Present Progressive Tense: (The students practise 
martial arts after school, don’t they? The canteen isn’t serving lunch now, is it? She can’t attend the Science 
club today, can she?)
