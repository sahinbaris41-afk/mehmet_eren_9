DIFFERENTIATION
Expansion
Objective: To talk about past and present routines in English through reading skills by using the target 
vocabulary.
Activity 1: Family Routines - Information Gap Reading
Students work in groups of 3-4. Each student receives a different part of a reading text about a family’s 
daily routines today and in the past (e.g., great-grandmother’s routine, sibling’s routine, etc.). The group is 
also given a blank table with headings like Family Member, Past Routine, and Present Routine. One student, 
who does not read the text, acts as the recorder and asks questions to the others (e.g., ''What did the great-
grandmother use to do in the morning?''). The others share information from their sections to fill in the 
table collaboratively. As groups work, the teacher moves around, listening to pronunciation and checking 
the accuracy of responses, and s/he provides supportive feedback. Students reflect on their thoughts and 
feelings about the activity.
Supporting
Objective: To compare past and present routines in English by using target vocabulary and target grammar.
Activity: Past and Present Routines
The teacher writes daily routines (e.g., wake up, vacuum the house) on papers and places them in a box. As 
an example, the teacher picks a paper (e.g., wake up), forms sentences comparing past and present routines 
using ''used to'' (e.g., ''I used to wake up at 6 am. Now, I wake up at 7 am'') and s/he writes them on the board. 
Then, students take turns picking a paper, forming similar sentences, and sharing them with the class. The 
teacher provides constructive and supportive feedback on accuracy and pronunciation. Students reflect on 
their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
