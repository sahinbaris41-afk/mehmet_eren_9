THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE CURRICULUM
Functions of the Target Grammatical Structures in Use:
-Use of the Simple Present vs. the Present Progressive for Future: Expressing scheduled or timetabled 
future events (Simple Present); expressing current intentions, ongoing plans, or arrangements for the near 
future. (Present Progressive).
Use of All Future Tenses (Mixed) (revisional): Expressing various degrees of certainty, intention, probability, 
and aspirations about future actions, events, and plans.
-Use of modal “Should” for Advice: Giving suggestions, advice, and recommendations about what is right or 
appropriate.
-Use of modal “Can” for Ability: Expressing past abilities, hypothetical situations, and future possibilities.
-Use of modal “Could” for Ability: Expressing present or future abilities, skills or possibilities.
-Use of Adjectives (Regular, -ing ending, -ed ending): Describing characteristics, emotions, and states 
(regular adjectives); describing ongoing actions or experiences (adjectives with -ing endings); describing 
feelings or states resulting from experiences (adjectives with -ed endings).
-Use of Comparative Adjectives (Regular, Irregular): Comparing two things in terms of greater or lesser 
degree.
-Use of “As…as, Same…as, Like, Alike”: Expressing similarity, comparison, and likeness between objects, 
actions, or people; describing similarities in personal habits.

Generalisations/Key Concepts  
Generalisations
The target concept of this theme ''personal life and well-being with its components such as body parts, 
human illnesses and diseases; human nature and human well-being today and in the future; fashion and 
personal fashion preferences'' is present in almost all languages. Students who start to learn a new language 
inevitably bring with them their pre-existing knowledge and transfer it to their new language learning context. 
Most example, a student in an upper secondary school may know what ''personal life and well-being with its 
components such as body parts, human illnesses and diseases; human nature and human well-being today 
and in the future; fashion and personal fashion preferences'' mean, but may not know how to say these things 
in their own language if this has not yet been covered in their existing curriculum. However, this existing 
knowledge in their own language will help them recognise, understand, and learn the new information more 
easily in a foreign language, in this case English.
Key Concepts
Personal life, well-being, body parts, human illnesses, diseases, human nature, human well-being today, 
human well-being in the future; fashion, personal fashion preferences.
