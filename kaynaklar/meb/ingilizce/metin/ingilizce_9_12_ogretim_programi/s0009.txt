Philosophical, Theoretical, and Methodological Stance of the English Language Curriculum 
Philosophical Approach to Learning and Teaching 
In the English Language Curriculum, the philosophical background does not refer to a background that 
defines foreign language education as the transfer of structures to the student, but to a background that 
aims at the individual, cultural and social perception, formation and development of the learner. In the English 
Language Curriculum, as in any field of education, the teaching of English as a foreign language is based on 
a number of philosophical approaches. This foundation includes the basic principles that reveal the goals, 
nature, organisation, and teaching processes of foreign language education. The theoretical approaches of 
applied linguistics, which establish the theoretical framework of teaching English as a foreign language, are 
also positioned on this philosophical background. In addition, the design, implementation, and evaluation of 
the curriculum are also shaped on this background.
The components of the philosophical foundation of the English Language Curriculum can be considered as 
“the aims and objectives of learning English”, “the process of learning English”.
a) The Aims and Objectives of Learning English
Individual formation and development goals that describe English language learning as a 
phenomenon contributes to cognitive, emotional and spiritual development and supports individual 
development not only for communication but also as a means of thinking and perceiving.
