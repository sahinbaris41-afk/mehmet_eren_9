ENG.11.2.S5.
RECONSTRUCTING/COMMUNICATING THROUGH SPEAKING: Collaborative and communicative activities are 
designed and used to revise and consolidate the whole theme on “comparing different learning activities and 
styles; comparing traditional education and modern education with technology” (V4.5, LS5, CS2.13, CS2.14). 
Storification, drama, and gamification techniques are actively and extensively used for further speaking 
practice throughout the theme (SELS2.1, SELS2.2, V4.1, V4.2, LS4, LS9). Students are encouraged to 
produce several spoken products related to the extended contexts such as dialogues, speeches, short texts, 
presentations, etc. to interact and to communicate with peers and/or other people inside and/or outside the 
school by using the language including the target words, language chunks, and social language expressions 
for the current theme (SELS2.3, V3.4, V7.1, V7.2, LS1, CS2.20). AI-based technologies can be used for 
various activities, giving students access to authentic materials. They can also produce digital materials and 
projects for speaking at school and at home through recordings (D3.3, SELS3.1, SELS3.2, V7.3, V11.1, LS2). 
All these speaking activities can be evaluated as described in items (1), (2), (3), (4), (5), (7), (8), (9), (10), and (11) in 
the section “1.2. Principles for the Implementation of the English Language Curriculum”.
ENG.11.2.S6.
REFLECTING THROUGH SPEAKING: Students are encouraged to reflect individually or collaboratively in 
groups on their own individual learning (SELS1.3) or on their collaborative learning in pairs, groups or as a 
whole class collaboratively (D2.3, SELS2.2, CS2.15). The activities will help and support students in these 
feedback and reflection sessions. Their feedback is shared with peers and the teacher and takes the form 
of interactive and ongoing feedback that can help their own improvement (SELS1.2, SELS2.1) and also they 
contribute to the improvement of their peers in their learning processes (V4.1, V4.3, V12.1, V12.3, LS1).
DIFFERENTIATION
Expansion
Objective: To write about designing a school program in English by using target vocabulary.
Activity 1: Design Your Dream School Programme
The teacher provides a worksheet with two tasks. In the first task, students match vocabulary with their 
definitions. For example, Students match ''collaborative'' with ''involving teamwork and cooperation''. In the 
second task, the teacher asks students to write a paragraph about a programme for a school of their choice, 
such as a ''Tech-Driven School'', ''Creative Arts School'', or ''Sports Academy''.  Students describe the activities, 
tools, and learning styles used in the school. After they finish, the teacher collects the papers, checks them, 
and gives written feedback on each, helping students improve their vocabulary and writing. Students reflect 
on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
Supporting
Objective: To recognise and understand different learning activities and styles in English through a matching 
and fill-in-the-blanks activity by using target vocabulary.
Activity 1: Match & Complete
The teacher provides a worksheet to the students. In the first section, students match pictures with the 
corresponding vocabulary words from a given box (e.g., textbooks, smart boards, handouts, distance 
learning, face-to-face learning). For example, a picture of a smart board is matched with the word ''smart 
board''. In the second section, students complete a fill-in-the-blanks activity using a word bank (collaborative, 
self-confident, workshop). Example sentences include: ''The teacher will organise a ______ project to 
improve teamwork skills'', or ''We will attend a ______ on digital learning tools tomorrow morning''. At the end 
of the activity, the teacher checks the answers with the class, explains the correct options, and provides 
constructive feedback. Students reflect on their thoughts and feelings about the activity.
