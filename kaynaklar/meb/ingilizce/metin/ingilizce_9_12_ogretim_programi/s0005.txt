THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE CURRICULUM
5
Alignment of the English Language Curriculum with the Common European Framework of 
Reference for Languages (CEFR)
The English Language Curriculum was aligned with the Common European Framework of Reference for 
Languages (CEFR) by comparing and matching the curriculum’s content and learning outcomes with the 
CEFR descriptors. The alignment process took into account the target main and sub-themes, vocabulary, 
language structures, and social language content, in line with course hours, the socio-cultural context, and 
the aim of preparing for the future of foreign language education in Türkiye.
CEFR level A1 has been adapted as A1.1, A1.2, A1.3 in Years 2, 3 and 4 for Primary Education. CEFR level A2 
has been adapted as A2.1, A2.2, A2.3, A2.4 in Years 5, 6, 7, and 8 for Lower Secondary Education. CEFR Level 
B1 has been adapted as B1.1, B1.2, B1.3, B1.4 in Years 9, 10, 11, and 12 for Upper Secondary Education. The 
English Language Curriculum for the Multilingual Foreign Language Programme includes CEFR Levels A2+B1 
in Year 5. CEFR Level B2 has been adapted as B2.1, B2.2, B2.3 levels in Years 6, 7 and 8 for Lower Secondary 
Education. The Upper Secondary School Preparatory Programme has been adapted to A2+ B1 levels. CEFR 
Level B2 has been adapted as B2.1, B2.2, B2.3, B2.4 levels in Years 9, 10, 11, and 12 for Upper Secondary 
Education after Preparatory Programme.
Four Field Skills and Three Supportive Skills in the English Language Curriculum
The English Language Curriculum is based on four field language skills (listening/watching-comprehension, 
reading-comprehension, speaking-expression, writing-expression) and additionally three supportive 
language skills (grammaring: selection-use, vocabulary: selection-use, and pronunciation: selection-use). 
Most of the time, “grammar knowledge, vocabulary knowledge, and knowledge of phonological aspects 
(pronunciation)” have been accepted as content knowledge in the language skills literature because they are 
based on the “recognition” stage of the receptive language skills. However, the “selection and use stages” of 
these skills should be clearly defined, and it should also be emphasised that they relate to the production 
stage of language. Otherwise, this gap in the literature may lead to confusion and misunderstandings. Once 
target grammatical items, target vocabulary and phonological aspects have been receptively recognised 
and identified in context as part of linguistic competence, they need to be selected and used accurately, 
appropriately, and efficiently in the interaction or communication phases involved in acquiring productive 
skills. This always happens in the natural flow of language practice however has not been precisely and openly 
named and defined in the foreign language literature. In the English Language Curriculum, the position, place, 
and application of these skills are clearly named, defined, and described to be consciously used in practise. 
The English Language Curriculum emphasises their importance without changing the status and importance 
of four field language skills recognising that all those field skills and these supportive skills have an 
interdependent, reciprocal, and effective relationship for language acquisition, learning, and development.
Terminology for Language Skills  
When referring to language skills, the listening skill is described and used as “listening/watching-
comprehension (meaning making) skill” rather than simply “listening”; while the reading skill is described as 
“reading-comprehension (meaning making) skill” rather than simply “reading” as receptive skills. Similarly, the 
writing skill is described as “writing-expression skill” instead of just “writing” and speaking skill is described as 
“speaking-expression skill” instead of just “speaking” as productive skills. The selection and use of grammar, 
vocabulary and pronunciation as supportive skills are described as “grammaring: selection-use skill, 
vocabulary: selection-use skill, and pronunciation: selection-use skill” in the English Language Curriculum.
