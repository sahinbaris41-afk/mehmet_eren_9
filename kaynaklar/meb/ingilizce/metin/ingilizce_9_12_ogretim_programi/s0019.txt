• The English Language Curriculum includes different contents that are consistent with the learning 
outcomes, taking into account the learners’ readiness and learning styles and strategies in a way 
appropriate to their language levels. Written materials such as puzzles, paragraphs, reading passages, 
short stories, novels, poems, articles, worksheets, magazines, reviews; visual materials such as 
illustrated-cartoon-anime-continuous stories, posters, picture cards; preparation and verbal/audio/
visual materials such as rhymes, songs, dialogues, games, simple story recitals, podcasts, documentaries, 
interviews, radio programmes, educational videos, story videos, short films, presentation contents, 
chat groups, simulations, animations, etc. have been suggested as sources for learning experiences. 
While preparing such learning-teaching materials, cooperation and collaboration should be provided by 
department teachers and other branch teachers.
• After the introduction of each theme is provided with a contextual and introductory digital introductory 
story (film), the flow of all the language skills are given a place in an integrated way. There is a unified 
design in the “Speaking-Expression” skill to accompany other skills throughout the theme. “Grammaring: 
Selection-Use, Vocabulary: Selection-Use, Pronunciation: Selection-Use” skills are used and practised 
with the “gamification” techniques and aimed to be used effectively in communication. The impact of 
such practice activities on learning should be taken into consideration.
• In The English Language Curriculum, the reading skill is of great importance because reading texts 
create a written context providing input and providing reception of the language. In primary school 
classes, group, vocal and fun “pyramid reading” and “radio reading” techniques, which are also used for 
pronunciation are preferred. In secondary school, “silent jigsaw reading” techniques, which are especially 
useful and effective for crowded classes are suggested. It should not be forgotten that reading should 
be vocal for pronunciation and silent for meaning. It should also be remembered that aloud reading in 
turns serves neither purpose and wastes time. Reading skills in The English Language Curriculum are 
studied through reading sections that include the written version of the digital introductory story and 
also various other reading passages.
• “Content and Language Integrated Learning (CLIL)” is one of the methodological components of English 
Language Curriculum. In CLIL, English is taught through other disciplines. The main aim is to teach 
both the content and the language together in a simultaneous way. Culture is also an important part of 
CLIL. Local, target, and international cultures can be learned through several contents by integrating 
them into CLIL activities. All language skills are proposed to be used for “CLIL” in The English Language 
Curriculum. Information processing, problem solving, researching, collaboration, critical thinking, and 
discussion techniques need to be used while working in CLIL. It must be seen as an important means 
of learning about one’s own and other people’s cultures, developing awareness of them; seeing new 
contextual uses of English created with other disciplines, and understanding and explaining content 
related to other cultures and disciplines in English.
• Consolidation is a significant process in English Language Curriculum. Content covering all grammatical 
items, target vocabulary, and social language is consolidated on a regular basis through enjoyable and 
meaningful activities. Ongoing cartoon-anime-picture-photo strip stories with resident characters are 
suggested to consolidate the content of the themes in a vivid context. All language skills are used to 
consolidate the content and to provide communication by producing new contents and contexts while 
revising the knowledge and skills acquired and remembered.
