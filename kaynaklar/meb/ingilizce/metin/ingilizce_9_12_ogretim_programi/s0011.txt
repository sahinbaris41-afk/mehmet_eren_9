Post-Method Approach
The English Language Curriculum does not rely on and adhere to a single language teaching method but 
synthesises various English language teaching approaches, methods defined and described by different 
approaches and theories, combining them by selecting and using appropriate techniques from them in 
a meaningful way. This combination has been designed in line with the basic objectives in all years of the 
English Language Curriculum. The aim of this combination is to facilitate learners’ learning processes so that 
they can develop their basic language skills, gain communicative competence and use English effectively, 
naturally, and competently in real and natural life contexts. This synthesis has been adopted in the English 
Language Curriculum and explains the methodological stance of it. 
This stance in the English Language Curriculum, is not based on a specific and single teaching method; it defines 
and describes the selection of the appropriate and suitable techniques and activities for the appropriate and 
suitable learner, place, time, context, situation and purpose, with an emphasis on factors affecting language 
acquisition and learning, such as learners’ age, learning styles, motivation levels, aptitudes, and personality 
traits, as described in the Post-Method Era Approach to teaching languages.
In the “Post-Method Era” perspective of the programme, various up-to-date and traditional methods such as 
“the Action-Oriented Method of CEFR”, “the Communicative Method”, “Blended Learning”, “Flipped Classroom”, 
“Situated Learning”, “Digital-Artificial Intelligence Supported Learning”, “Contextual and Authentic Learning”, 
“Social Learning”, “Collaborative Learning”, “Learning Through Drama”, “Task-Based Language Learning 
Design”, “Total Physical Response”, “Community-Language Learning”, “Multiple Intelligence Theory”,  and 
“Suggestopedia”, are considered as part of the approach. “Field skills”, “learning outcomes” and “learning-
teaching experiences” sections in the English Language Curriculum have been organised in accordance 
with this methodological background. The practical implementation of the English Language Curriculum is 
planned according to this theoretical and academic stance by taking learners’ ages; cognitive, emotional, 
physical, social, cultural aspects; learning styles; and interests into account.
Socio-Cultural Approach and the Communicative Approach
In the design and formation of the English Language Curriculum, one of the basic foundations is the “Socio- 
Cultural Approach” which posits that language acquires meaning within social and cultural contexts, and that 
language learning enables individuals to interact with different cultures while maintaining their own cultural 
identity. This approach is based on the interaction between language and culture and fosters intercultural 
understanding. This approach has been proposed and developed by many applied linguists and has been 
adopted as one of the central conceptual and theoretical foundations of foreign language education. 
English can make important contributions to both individuals and societies in terms of their personal 
development, access to information, various personal, educational, professional and business opportunities, 
their ability to understand and convey cultural differences, and participation in universal communication. 
Today, it is widely used as a global means of communication among individuals and societies in a 
number of fields such as education, science, the arts, technology, business, media, and diplomacy.
Through different forms and varieties of English, it can enable speaking communities by articulating 
speaking communities to express their own identities and can allow different individual and local voices to 
be heard in global communication contexts rather than dominating with its culture and nativeness through 
it changing role in the world. In creating themes and course contents of the English Language Curriculum, 
the “Socio-Cultural Approach” has been adopted as part of the conceptual and theoretical framework, 
emphasising that English can serve as a common means for people to express their social and cultural voices.
