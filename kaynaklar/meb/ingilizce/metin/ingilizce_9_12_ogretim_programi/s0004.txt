1. THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE CURRICULUM 
1.1. THE BASIC APPROACH AND SPECIFIC AIMS OF THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE CURRICULUM 
The Ultimate Goal of the English Language Curriculum
The fundamental goal and target of the English Language Curriculum of the Century of Türkiye Education 
Model is to enable and empower learners to acquire competence in the use of English language so that they 
can communicate effectively, competently, confidently, authentically, and naturally in all Communities of 
Practice (CoPs) and contexts that require the use of English, by supporting them according to their needs, 
ages, levels, interests, styles and aptitudes.
In this perspective, the aims and objectives of the English Language Curriculum are to support learners in 
acquiring and using all the knowledge and skills effectively and successfully so as to express themselves 
and understand others accurately and appropriately in all social and cultural contexts that require the use of 
English. In this way, it aims to support learners in successful use of English as a tool, medium, and means to 
make positive and progressive contributions to life in their own society, to life in the world, and to life in the 
future.
The Position of English for Türkiye
Spoken by billions of people around the world and used in science, education, the arts, the environment, 
health, tourism, business, etc., English covers a large geography as a mother tongue, second-administrative/ 
official language and foreign language. Therefore, it is considered to be the most common language in the 
world in terms of the number of individuals who speak it, the third most common language in terms of the 
number of its native speakers, and the most widespread language being used in a geography covering almost 
90 countries and regions. This situation facilitates ease of communication at international level.
The global status and role of English are going a significant change. Once regarded and viewed as culturally 
dominant language, English now serves as an international medium that can help countries to convey their 
unique circumstances, cultures, and lived experiences from their own socio-cultural perspectives. In the 
English Language Curriculum, the term “foreign language” is preferred for the English language. This term is 
based on the three concentric “Language Circles/Circle Model” which summarises the use of English around 
the world. According to this model, the “Inner Circle”, includes countries such as the United Kingdom and the 
United States of America, where English is used as a “mother tongue/first language/ native language”. They 
are known as the counties that set the standard criteria for the English language, and as countries where 
people learn and use it as a “second language”. These countries are home to various people and communities 
whose first language is not English. The “Outer Circle” are former British colonies where English is used as 
a “second/official/administrative language”. The “Expanding Circle” countries where English is learnt and 
used as a “foreign language” and it is not the native language of the learners and users in these countries. 
According to this classification, Türkiye is in the “Expanding Circle” and is a country where English is taught 
as a “foreign language”.
The Attributes that Constitute the Unique Character of the English Language Curriculum  
There are several attributes which give the English Language Curriculum its unique character as described 
below.
The Focus on the Aspects and Factors of Language Learning  
The learners’ learning types, resulting from their cognitive, emotional, physical, social and learning culture 
aspects, and also factors of learning such as “age, aptitude, learning styles, motivation level, and personality”, 
were taken into account in each dimension of the English Language Curriculum which includes “field skills”, 
“learning outcomes”, “learning-teaching experiences and practices”, and “differentiated learning practices”. 
In the Years 9-12 English Language Curriculum, the “four field skills and three supportive skills”, ‘‘learning 
outcomes’’, and “learning-teaching experiences and practices” were designed and developed according to an 
“inductive and discovery (consciousness raising)-based” approach.
