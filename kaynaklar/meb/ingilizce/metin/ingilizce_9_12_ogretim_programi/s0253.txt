Target Grammatical Structures in Use:
-The Simple Future Tense: (The teacher will assign a brainstorming activity tomorrow.)
-Be Going to Future Tense: (I’m going to summarise the main points of the debate for our next class.)
-Question tags: (We should always collaborate on assignments, shouldn’t we? The students will log in to the 
website for their group work, won’t they?)
-Should & ought to for obligation: (Students should share their ideas during brainstorming sessions to 
improve collaboration. You ought to take notes while the teacher is explaining the concept map.)
-Must-have to for prohibition and obligation: (You must not share your password with anyone. The teacher 
said we have to upload our assignments by 5 p.m.)
-Reflexive Pronouns: (You should ask yourself if you are using the right method to solve the problem. The 
students taught themselves how to use the AI-based learning tool.)
-Can for polite requests, permission, probability, and possibility: (Can I access the homepage to download the 
assignments? The students can collaborate more effectively by using the interactive activity.)
-Might for polite requests and probability: (Might you show me how to use the printer for the concept mapping 
task? The laptop might not connect to the server due to a weak signal.)
-Possessive pronouns: (The laptop on the table is mine, but the printer is yours. The summary is complete, 
but we still need to check if the solution is theirs.)
-Possessive (‘s): (The teacher’s instructions were clear about how to define the concept. The student’s laptop 
crashed while they were trying to edit the file.)
Functions of the Target Grammatical Structures in Use:
-Use of the Simple Future Tense: Making predictions, decisions, promises, or offers made at the moment of 
speaking; predicting upcoming events.
-Use of “Be Going to” Future Tense: Describing plans, intentions, or events based on evidence or prior 
decisions; describing planned activities and personal learning.
-Use of the Simple Future Tense vs. Be going to: Expressing the difference between immediate and planned 
future actions and events.
-Use of “Question Tags”: Confirming or clarifying information in a polite or engaging way; inviting agreement
