b) The Process of Learning English
In the English Language Curriculum, the process of learning English refers to individual formation, 
growth, and development. The process dimension is reflected in the English Language Curriculum 
through various “Applied Linguistics” approaches. This theoretical background has been synthesised 
in the teaching of English as a foreign language in all English classes and themes of the programme, 
and this synthesised theoretical background has formed the background and framework for defining 
the content and implementation of the English Language Curriculum.
Action-Oriented Approach to Foreign Language Learning-Teaching Process in the English Language 
Curriculum
One of the major principles of the Action-Oriented Approach (AOA) is described as enabling learners to 
perform for succeeding in communication in real-life socio-cultural contexts as stated in the 2020 version of 
the CEFR Handbook. Learners’ involvement in the learning process and their awareness on the social nature 
and use of language learning is of great importance in AOA. Learners are expected to be aware of their 
own individual learning and also of the relationship between the consequences of their personal learning 
and the social life. These principles are in harmony with the academic stance of the English Language 
Curriculum which is based on these autonomous, purposeful, communicative, and collaborative learning in 
socio-cultural contexts in and out of the school. These characteristics of the English Language Curriculum 
direct the attention from the language itself to “how to learn and how to use it” perspective in the AOA as 
stated in the CEFR. Linguistic elements are the means of English language learning to be able to use them 
autonomously and collaboratively with peers and other people in social and cultural contexts. In the English 
Language Curriculum, the aim for learners is to interact and communicate successfully, but then to transfer 
their knowledge and skills as successful communicators contributing to their own development, to their own 
society in which they live in and also to the whole world through English as their foreign language, wherever 
the use of English language is needed.
To bring these ideas to life, each sub-theme of the main theme in the English Language Curriculum is used 
to build up a micro socio-cultural context with all possible real-life situations through relevant materials and 
bring them into the classroom. Learners first learn what these concepts in the sub-themes are about with 
the help of linguistics elements such as words, phrases, sentences, use of grammatical items and so on. 
Then they understand and learn how these linguistic elements function in creating semantic meanings in 
English. They learn how to use them in communicating with other people to express their own understandings 
through their knowledge, thoughts, ideas, and feelings, and then to understand the perspective of other 
people in English. Ultimately, the aim of the English Language Curriculum for learners is to transform their 
communicative competence, capacity, and ability to be good communicators and contributors in their 
community, in different (multilingual) societies, and in the world to make it a better place.
In the English Language Curriculum, this transformation is processed through several communicative 
activities, tasks, projects, assignments, and performances throughout the themes. Learners work on many 
activities and tasks to learn how to interact and communicate, then they transfer this knowledge and skills to 
where they work both individually and collaboratively on real-life topics and socio-cultural contexts of each 
theme of the English Language Curriculum to access issues, challenges, problems and their remedies about 
the facts and situations in their immediate or distant environment.
