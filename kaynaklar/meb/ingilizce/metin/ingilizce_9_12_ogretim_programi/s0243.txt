REFLECTING THROUGH SPEAKING: Students are encouraged to reflect individually or collaboratively in 
groups on their own individual learning (SELS1.3) or on their collaborative learning in pairs, groups or as a 
whole class collaboratively (D2.3, SELS2.2, CS2.15). The activities will help and support students in these 
feedback and reflection sessions. Their feedback is shared with peers and the teacher and takes the form 
of interactive and ongoing feedback that can help their own improvement (SELS1.2, SELS2.1) and also they 
contribute to the improvement of their peers in their learning processes (V4.1, V4.3, V12.1, V12.3, LS1).
DIFFERENTIATION
Expansion
Objective: To talk about regular and extracurricular activities at different types of schools, school anxiety, 
and stress in English through a discussion activity by using target vocabulary and target grammar.
Activity 1: Pick, Discuss, and Present
The teacher informs students that they will work in small groups to discuss school-related topics. The teacher 
writes discussion questions, such as ''How often do students participate in extracurricular activities at your 
school?'' and ''What facilities must a school have to reduce stress?'' on small pieces of paper and places them 
in a box. Each group sends one student to pick a question from the box. The group discusses the selected 
question, with each member giving a different answer using adverbs of frequency (e.g., Students usually 
participate in sports) and deduction (e.g., The gym must be an important facility). After the discussion, the 
group writes down their answers and reads them aloud to the class. The teacher provides feedback on 
pronunciation and correct use of the target grammar. Students reflect on their thoughts and feelings about 
the activity.
Supporting
Objective: To talk about regular and extracurricular activities at different types of schools, school anxiety, 
and stress in English through a matching activity by using target vocabulary.
Activity 1: Match & Learn
The teacher provides students with a worksheet including pictures and school-related vocabulary words 
(e.g., cafeteria, gym, exams, soccer, stress) or uses a web 2.0 tool to design the matching activity. Students 
work in pairs to match each picture with the correct English word by drawing lines, writing the answers, or 
interacting with the digital tool. After completing the activity, they take turns reading the words aloud to 
practice pronunciation. The teacher gives constructive and supportive feedback. Students reflect on their 
thoughts and feelings about the activity.
